Methodology 1 Machine Learning · Hurdle Framework

LightGBM Two-Stage: Decoupled Hurdle Framework

Separating order occurrence from transaction volume to eliminate zero-demand distortion.

Layman's Intuition: The Two Gatekeepers

Imagine a warehouse loading dock managed by two gatekeepers:
• Gatekeeper 1 (The Screener): Asks only: "Is a purchase order coming today at all?" (Yes/No). Dark stores frequently skip ordering when inventory is above trigger.
• Gatekeeper 2 (The Sizer): Only called if Gatekeeper 1 says "Yes": "Assuming an order is placed, how many units will they take?"
Why it matters: Forcing one model to do both makes it guess fractional averages (e.g. 14 units every day), leaving you overstocked on quiet days and out of stock on delivery days. Decoupling solves this completely.

Operational Role & Win Share

Won 44 of 96 series (45.8%). Primary champion for fast-moving Personal Care hero SKUs on Blinkit and high-turnover dark-store fulfillment loops. Holdout WAPE: 8.30%.

Mathematical Construct & Formulations

1. Hurdle Probability Decomposition:
E[Y_t | X_t] = P(Y_t > 0 | X_t) · E[Y_t | Y_t > 0, X_t]

2. Stage 1 Binary Logistic Classification:
• Target: z_i = I(y_i > 0) ∈ {0, 1}
• Loss: L_cls = -∑ [z_i log(p_i) + (1 - z_i) log(1 - p_i)]
• Probability: p_i = σ(f_cls(x_i)) = 1 / (1 + e^-f(x))

3. Stage 2 Positive Continuous Regression:
• Loss: Huber Loss on {i : y_i > 0} (outlier-resistant)

4. Algorithmic Mechanics:
• GOSS (Gradient One-Side Sampling) retains top gradients and subsamples low gradients.
• Single-class bypass when nunique(z) == 1 (100% positive).
Methodology 2 Machine Learning · 2nd-Order Boosting

XGBoost Regressor: Extreme Gradient Tree Boosting

Second-order Taylor optimization on log-demand to master non-linear promotional surges.

Layman's Intuition: The Relentless Error-Correction Team

Imagine an elite team of golf pros attempting a difficult 500-yard shot in heavy wind:
• Pro 1 hits the first shot. It gets close, but lands 30 yards to the right.
• Pro 2 does NOT start over from the tee. Pro 2 stands where the ball landed, analyzes the remaining 30-yard error, and hits a precision correction shot.
• Pro 3 analyzes whatever tiny 2-foot error remains and taps it in.
Why it matters: Each tree in XGBoost exists solely to correct the errors of all prior trees. If Tree 1 underestimates a Diwali surge by 1,000 units, Tree 2 focuses heavily on that shortfall.

Operational Role & Win Share

Won 31 of 96 series (32.3%). Dominates promotional Pharmaceutical lines and steep festival ramps on Amazon & Flipkart. Holdout WAPE: 8.48%, R²: 0.9269.

Mathematical Construct & Formulations

1. Second-Order Taylor Objective Function:
Obj^(t) ≈ ∑ [ l(y_i, ŷ_i^(t-1)) + g_i·f_t(x_i) + 0.5·h_i·f_t(x_i)² ] + Ω(f_t)
• Gradient: g_i = ∂l(y_i, ŷ_i^(t-1)) / ∂ŷ_i^(t-1)
• Hessian: h_i = ∂²l(y_i, ŷ_i^(t-1)) / ∂(ŷ_i^(t-1))²

2. Regularized Tree Complexity Penalty:
Ω(f_t) = γ·T + 0.5·λ·∑ w_j² (γ prunes, λ shrinks)

3. Optimal Leaf Weight & Split Gain:
w_j* = - (∑ g_i) / (∑ h_i + λ)
Gain = 0.5·[ G_L²/(H_L+λ) + G_R²/(H_R+λ) - (G_L+G_R)²/(H_L+H_R+λ) ] - γ

4. Log-Demand Target Stabilization:
z = log(1 + y), predicted ŷ = max(0, exp(ẑ) - 1).
Methodology 3 Machine Learning · Bagged Ensemble

Random Forest: Bootstrap Aggregation & Subspace Voting

150 decorrelated decision trees delivering extreme resistance to outlier noise.

Layman's Intuition: The Jury of 150 Independent Analysts

When making a multi-million-unit inventory commitment, would you trust one single brilliant planner, or a jury of 150 independent supply chain analysts?
Random Forest is the "Wisdom of the Crowds":
1. It builds 150 independent decision trees.
2. Each tree is trained on a different random slice of historical sales data.
3. At every branch, each tree only looks at a random subset of features (e.g. Tree 1 evaluates CFAs and payday cycles; Tree 2 evaluates weekend surges and 4-week sales).
Taking their consensus average ensures single-order anomalies get out-voted.

Operational Role & Win Share

Won 18 of 96 series (18.8%). Ideal for volatile regional CFAs (e.g. Kolkata CFA) with sporadic ordering patterns. Holdout WAPE: 8.70%, R²: 0.9260.

Mathematical Construct & Formulations

1. Bootstrap Aggregation (Bagging):
From training set D of size N, draw B = 150 bootstrap samples D_b with replacement. Each sample contains ~63.2% unique instances.

2. Random Subspace Feature Selection:
At each split node, randomly sample m = ⌊p / 3⌋ features from total p features to decorrelate tree structures.

3. Node Partitioning via Variance Reduction:
ArgMax_{j,s} [ Var(S) - ( (|S_L|/|S|)Var(S_L) + (|S_R|/|S|)Var(S_R) ) ]

4. Ensemble Consensus Prediction & Variance Proof:
f̂_RF(x) = (1 / B) · ∑ T_b(x)
Var(f̂_RF) = ρ·σ² + ((1 - ρ) / B)·σ² → ρ·σ²
Random subspace selection intentionally drives down pair-wise tree correlation ρ, drastically suppressing prediction variance.
Methodology 4 Classical Time Series · State-Space

Holt-Winters Smoothing: Triple Exponential State-Space

Adaptive level, trend, and seasonal decomposition with damping for steady baseline series.

Layman's Intuition: The Adaptive Rearview Mirror

Imagine driving forward while navigating primarily by looking in your rearview mirror:
• What happened 2 days ago is crystal clear and very influential.
• What happened 6 months ago is small and blurry in the distance.
Holt-Winters constantly balances three dials:
1. Level Dial: Where is baseline demand sitting right now?
2. Trend Dial: Is demand sloping upward or downward, and by how much each period?
3. Seasonality Dial: What recurring day-of-week rhythm repeats over time?
A Damping Factor (φ) flattens the trend curve so it doesn't fly off to infinity.

Operational Role & Win Share

Won 3 of 96 series (3.1%). Best for mature, non-promotional Baby Care SKUs with steady repeat consumption. Holdout WAPE: 16.73% (pure univariate baseline without external feature access).

Mathematical Construct & Formulations

1. Recursive State Equations (Damped Additive):
• Level Equation:
ℓ_t = α·(y_t - s_{t-m}) + (1 - α)·(ℓ_{t-1} + φ·b_{t-1})

• Trend Equation:
b_t = β·(ℓ_t - ℓ_{t-1}) + (1 - β)·φ·b_{t-1}

• Seasonal Equation:
s_t = γ·(y_t - ℓ_{t-1} - φ·b_{t-1}) + (1 - γ)·s_{t-m}

2. Multi-Step-Ahead Forecast Function (h steps):
ŷ_{t+h|t} = ℓ_t + [ ∑_{i=1}^h φ^i ]·b_t + s_{t+h - m(k+1)}
where k = ⌊(h - 1) / m⌋.

3. Smoothing Parameters:
α, β, γ ∈ [0, 1] (state update speeds); φ ∈ (0, 1) (damping rate). In-sample fitted values extracted via ŷ_t = ŷ_{t|t-1}.
Methodology 5 Classical Econometrics · Exogenous ARMA

SARIMAX: Seasonal ARIMA with Exogenous Regressors

Combining autoregressive momentum, moving-average shocks, and promotional calendar flags.

Layman's Intuition: Momentum, Echoes, and External Levers

Imagine measuring ocean waves outside a harbor entrance:
• Momentum (AutoRegression - AR): If a massive wave hit 10 seconds ago, another wave is likely rolling in right behind it.
• Echoes of Shocks (Moving Average - MA): If a speedboat zoomed past 2 minutes ago, the ripples from that surprise shock bounce around before settling.
• Baseline Differencing (Integration - I): If the tide rises, you subtract the tide level to isolate the wave height.
• The 'X' Factor (eXogenous Variables): What if you know the harbor floodgate opens every Friday? The 'X' links demand spikes directly to planned promotional calendars.

Operational Role & Benchmark Value

Provides a rigorous econometric benchmark to test whether demand follows linear autoregressive momentum. Holdout WAPE: 16.73%, R²: 0.7168.

Mathematical Construct & Formulations

1. SARIMAX(p, d, q) × (P, D, Q)_s Model Specification:
Φ_P(B^s)·φ_p(B)·(1 - B)^d·(1 - B^s)^D·(y_t - β&supT; X_t) = Θ_Q(B^s)·θ_q(B)·ε_t

2. Operator Polynomials:
• Backshift: B^k·y_t = y_{t-k}
• Non-seasonal AR: φ_p(B) = 1 - ∑_{i=1}^p φ_i B^i
• Non-seasonal MA: θ_q(B) = 1 + ∑_{j=1}^q θ_j B^j
• Seasonal AR: Φ_P(B^s) = 1 - ∑_{i=1}^P Φ_i B^{i·s}
• Seasonal MA: Θ_Q(B^s) = 1 + ∑_{j=1}^Q Θ_j B^{j·s}

3. Exogenous Linear Shift (β&supT; X_t):
Incorporates festival surge indicators and promo flags.

4. Kalman Filter Estimation:
Parameters estimated via State-Space Maximum Likelihood: LogL = -0.5·∑ [ log|F_t| + v_t&supT; F_t^-1 v_t ].
Methodology 6 Meta-Optimization · Production Champion

Best Model Ensemble: Dynamic Champion-Challenger Routing

Per-series empirical risk minimization dynamically assigning the winning model to each node.

Layman's Intuition: The Specialized Olympic Team

In the Olympics, you never enter a 100m sprinter into a weightlifting contest. You field the best athlete for each specific event.
The Best-Model Ensemble adopts this strategy across Himalaya's supply chain:
• Fast-moving skincare on Blinkit (weekend surges): Two-Stage LightGBM wins gold.
• Medium-volume baby care on Amazon (coupon promotions): XGBoost wins gold.
• Intermittent high-variance SKU-CFA pairs: Random Forest wins gold.
Rather than forcing a single corporate formula across all 96 series, the system conducts a blind tournament on holdout data and routes the forecast to whoever won that series.

Primary Production Recommendation

Achieves 7.42% Holdout WAPE and 0.9449 R², outperforming the single best standalone model (LightGBM at 8.30%) by an absolute +0.88% WAPE (+10.6% relative error reduction).

Mathematical Construct & Formulations

1. Per-Series Champion Selection Problem:
Let S be the set of 96 discrete series, and M = {LGBM, XGB, RF, HW, SARIMAX}.
For each series s ∈ S, the champion M*(s) is selected as:
M*(s) = ArgMin_{m ∈ M} WAPE_s^(val)(m)

2. Out-of-Sample Objective Metric:
WAPE_s^(val)(m) = ( ∑_{t ∈ T_val} |y_{s,t} - ŷ_{s,t}(m)| ) / ( ∑_{t ∈ T_val} y_{s,t} )

3. Production Forecast Generation:
ŷ_{s,t+h} = ŷ_{s,t+h}(M*(s)) ∀ h ∈ {1,...,13}

4. Empirical Win Distribution Across 96 Series:
• LightGBM Two-Stage: 44 series won (45.8%)
• XGBoost Regressor: 31 series won (32.3%)
• Random Forest: 18 series won (18.8%)
• Holt-Winters Smoothing: 3 series won (3.1%)

1. Strict Temporal Splits & Pre-Shifted Lags

Zero lookahead leakage. Training (93 wks) and holdout (12 wks) are strictly chronologically partitioned. All rolling windows (`roll_mean_4`, `roll_mean_8`) are pre-shifted (`shift(1)`), guaranteeing $y_t$ is never inside the feature set.

2. Decoupled Two-Stage Modeling Architecture

Stage 1 Classifier predicts order incidence $P(\text{order} > 0)$, while Stage 2 Regressor estimates conditional volume. This structural separation protects regression trees from zero-demand noise and suppresses variance.

3. Tree Depth & Subsampling Regularization

Tree depth strictly capped (`max_depth = 5-6`, `min_child_samples = 15`), feature/row subsampling at 80%, and L2 leaf shrinkage (`reg_lambda = 1.0`) directly prevent memorizing single historical spikes.

4. Semantic Demand Drivers over Date Memorization

Models learn causal behavioral mechanisms—28th-5th payday salary cycles, Friday-Sunday dark-store replenishment waves, and 7-day pre-festival stocking lead times—enabling stable generalization to future periods.

Cross-Granularity Stability: As granularity tightens from Weekly → 5-Day → 3-Day, Holdout WAPE increases modestly (7.4% → 9.7% → 10.5%) while Holdout R² stays robustly above 0.91, proving the architecture is stable across micro and macro horizons.
